# Gift reaction research audit

## Audit consistency postmortem and required workflow (2026-10-10)

Recent audits changed gift records inconsistently because they mixed three kinds of evidence: exact item reactions, broad profile/category recommendations, and guides that collapse or disagree about reaction tiers. They also changed entries before comparing multiple sources. The Sha Lan audit is a concrete example: category advice about cooking and tea was conflated with exact tiers; Game8 was used alone to add candidates and downgrade Special “Medicine”; GameWith and Raider King were checked only after the user objected. This produced avoidable reversals and undermined confidence. That was a process failure, not evidence that the underlying reactions had changed.

Apply this workflow to every future audit:

1. **Preserve the current record while researching.** Prepare a proposed delta first; do not edit `gifts.ts` during initial source gathering.
2. **Check at least two independent sources for every public-source change.** Both must support the exact character, exact item, and relevant reaction tier. A broad preference category can guide candidate searches but does not count as exact-pair corroboration. User-confirmed in-game reactions are authoritative and do not require a second source.
3. **Check source independence.** Syndicated/reposted articles, derivative databases, and exports of the same source count as one source. Record provenance/source ancestry rather than counting URLs.
4. **Keep tiers separate.** Translate source tiers using the documented tier rule. A general recommendation such as “cooking gifts” or “fishing gifts” cannot establish Really Liked or Loved for every item in that category. Family-based expansions remain unverified until each exact reaction has adequate support.
5. **Resolve conflicts before changing a tier.** If two sources disagree, inspect their methods, exact labels, and dates. If the conflict remains unresolved, preserve the existing tier and mark the pair as disputed/unverified in the research notes; do not choose whichever source was checked most recently. Direct in-game user confirmations override guide conflicts.
6. **Treat omissions as unknown.** No source listing an item is not evidence that the character dislikes it. Do not remove a pair solely because it is absent from a partial list.
7. **Record the change and its basis together.** For each changed pair, note old state → new state, sources and their independence, tier mapping, and any remaining uncertainty. Keep user confirmations explicitly identified so later audits cannot downgrade them based on guide omissions.

These rules are intended to prevent audit-driven churn. An audit should not silently rewrite neighboring pairs: only researched, evidence-supported deltas should change.

## Why confirmed pairs were missing

The original research basis was too narrow: a WIP community reaction table and a second community compilation with only partial roster coverage. Their omissions were treated as if the source pass had been broad enough. It had not. A wider comparison found useful Japanese guides and a per-character item database, but these also have distinct limits:

- [Raider King's reaction table](https://raiderking.com/fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-all-loved-gifts-guide/) separates the game's one-arrow and two-arrow reactions and marks some guesses, but calls itself a work in progress. It omits several pairs subsequently confirmed in-game by the user.
- [GameWith's table](https://gamewith.jp/fefw/577115) says it lists gifts it has confirmed and maps its top `大好き` tier to the in-game “really liked” notification. It includes exact item entries and broad item families, but that notification alone does not distinguish one support arrow from two.
- [Game8's table](https://game8.jp/fe-banshisenko/816847) separates the in-game `とても気に入った` and `気に入った` labels, but not the one-arrow/two-arrow distinction used by this app. Its published coverage has explicit “no data” entries.
- [Altema's table](https://altema.jp/febansisenkou/sukinamono) gives extensive item-level preferences, but does not document a method that separates the two arrow counts. Treat it as corroboration for a preference, not definitive proof of this app's Loved tier.
- [Redfreshet's per-character database](https://redfreshet.com/game-tools/fe-banshisenko/characters/) distinguishes profile hints from individual item reactions and says its specific reaction entries are game-checked. Its published reaction coverage is still partial; an absent pair is not evidence of a negative reaction.
- The [Fortune's Weave community gift table](https://fortunesweave.co.uk/wiki/gifts) and [Raider King comments](https://www.reddit.com/r/FE_Fortunes_Weave/comments/1wpxbom/all_loved_gifts_guide/) add reported observations, but share incomplete community coverage. Some third-party guides, including [Nintendo Insider](https://www.nintendo-insider.com/fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-characters-gifts-likes-and-interests/) and [Keengamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/guides/fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-best-gifts-guide/), map interests to gift categories rather than report exact reactions.

That explains the failure: checking two incomplete compilations and extrapolating from character/item categories gave false confidence about coverage. Several of the new pairs are absent from the WIP matrix; others appear only as a category-level preference in Japanese tables. In particular, GameWith's table includes Simon with Pickled Vegetables, while Raider King's table lists Pickled Bulbs but not Pickled Vegetables. Altema explicitly lists Honey Pastries for Halvin, while Raider King's Halvin row lists other pastry types. These are examples of why absence from one list cannot be used to reject an observed pair.

## Tier rule and remaining coverage limits

The available public sources do not provide a complete, authoritative matrix with both exact item and one-arrow/two-arrow reaction. Use the positive-tier evidence they do provide: Japanese `大好き` / `とても気に入った` entries establish the top positive reaction. When the source cannot establish whether that earns one or two arrows, record it as Really Liked; reserve Loved for two-arrow evidence. This preserves confirmed strong reactions without overstating the Loved tier.

Broad profile interests and item-description categories alone are not exact reactions. Keep category-only expansions marked low confidence until checked in-game or corroborated by an exact item reaction source. Absence remains unknown, not disliked. The remaining research task is to continue comparing the unrepresented roster and exact item entries against these sources, applying the lower-tier rule wherever the top positive reaction is known but the arrow count is not.

## Gift-family inference and directionality (2026-10-04)

### Working rule

Record exact character/item reactions as the source of truth. A confirmed preference for a broad family is a strong directional clue that sibling variants will also be positive, but it is not proof that every variant has the same reaction tier. A confirmed preference for one named variant does not justify expanding that reaction to its whole family. In particular, never infer “likes all Ghosh” from liking Southern Ghosh alone. Keep inferred exact pairs marked `unverified` until checked in-game or established by an exact item reaction source.

This matches the user's in-game observations so far: they have not found a counterexample to general/common family affinity extending positively to distinctive variants, and have observed the reverse not to hold. The public sources reviewed here show the same directional pattern and no explicit contrary example, but their coverage is incomplete; treat “likely, perhaps nearly universal” as a strong working heuristic, not a proven game mechanic.

### Family vocabulary

- **Fermented drinks** is the broad description family. It includes **Ghosh** and **Shosh**, which remain separate subfamilies for tracking. The Ghosh variants in this catalogue are Young, Mature, Sun, Energizing, Rustic, Jade, Secret, Southern, Volcano, and Crimson Ghosh. Shosh variants are Young, Mature, Light, Blue, and Aromatic Shosh.
- **Training gifts** is a practical family for Training Weights, Training Pillar, and Training Bracelet. Keep this separate from the character-profile interest “training” or “鍛錬” until an item description or exact reaction establishes the scope: a profile interest is a family-level clue, not an exact item reaction.
- Other source-described gift families (books, pastries, coffee, etc.) may be tracked by the same rule, but only where the item's in-game description or an explicit source category defines membership. Similar names or themes alone are insufficient grounds to add an item to a family.

### Evidence checked

- [GameWith's preference table](https://gamewith.jp/fefw/577115) explicitly uses broad categories, including “fermented drinks (such as Young Ghosh)” and “training gifts (such as Training Weights).” Its character profile-interest lists include drinking/alcohol and training. This supports using the game's stated category as a family-level predictor. It does not give exact reactions for every sibling or the Loved-versus-Really-Liked arrow count.
- [Game8's character-specific gift tables](https://game8.jp/fe-banshisenko/816847) separate the strongest, weaker, and somewhat-positive reactions by exact item. [Guzran's table](https://game8.jp/fe-banshisenko/816440), for example, places several distinct Ghosh variants across positive tiers: Mature, Crimson, and Volcano in the strongest tier; variants including Jade, Secret, Southern, Sun, Rustic, and Young in a weaker tier. That is broad positive coverage with tier variation, rather than evidence that every variant earns the same response. [Jester's table](https://game8.jp/fe-banshisenko/816452) likewise puts Crimson Ghosh and Mature Ghosh in different positive tiers. These examples support separating “positive family affinity” from “identical tier for all members.”
- [Raider King's WIP reaction table](https://raiderking.com/fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-all-loved-gifts-guide/) labels broad groups such as “All Ghosh” / “Any Ghosh” separately from individually named variants, and places some characters' distinctive variants in different positive columns from the general group. The author says `?` marks pairs not personally verified, so use it as corroboration, not a complete authority. Examples include Buccar (broad Ghosh/Shosh affinity with Volcano and Crimson Ghosh listed at the top tier), Leda (Any Ghosh with Volcano/Crimson at the top tier), and Jester (Any Ghosh stronger than some named Ghosh variants). Again, reaction strength can differ while the variants remain positive.
- The inspected tables do not establish a fully tested universal implication. Their omissions cannot prove dislike or disconfirm the directional rule. A unique-variant preference is therefore evidence for that exact variant only; without a separately stated family category or checks of sibling items, do not fill in the family.

### Data handling

Keep family membership and exact reactions conceptually distinct. When a source confirms a general family preference, use it to prioritize sibling checks and, if useful, record sibling pairs as low-confidence leads. Do not silently turn a broad category into verified exact pairs or promote every sibling to the same tier. When only one unusual variant is confirmed, retain only that pair.
